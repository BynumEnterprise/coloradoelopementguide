Sapphire Point is the overlook you have seen in a thousand Colorado elopement galleries: a rocky point in the lodgepole pines on Swan Mountain Road, Dillon Reservoir spread out below, the Gore and Tenmile ranges filling the horizon. It is also one of the few Colorado ceremony sites you can actually reserve, which makes it the default choice for couples who want a high mountain view without hiking, without a national park permit, and less than two hours from Denver.

It is a small, shared, rule bound site. Know the rules and it is wonderful. Ignore them and you will be the couple with 45 guests and no parking.

## Where it is

Sapphire Point Overlook sits at about 9,500 feet in the White River National Forest, between Keystone and Breckenridge. From Interstate 70, take exit 203 for Frisco and Breckenridge and drive south on Highway 9 for about 4.9 miles. Turn left onto Swan Mountain Road at the Farmer's Korner intersection, then climb about 1.8 miles to the parking area on the left. From Keystone, Swan Mountain Road leaves US 6 to the west and reaches the lot in about four miles.

A short trail leads from the lot to the overlooks. There are no restrooms and no drinking water at the site. In winter the path is not maintained, so expect ice and dress your guests for it.

## The new north overlook

This is the change most older guides miss. The Forest Service says that beginning June 14, 2026, all reservations at Sapphire Point are for the new north overlook, and the old west overlook, the one with the rock wall, is no longer used for reservations or events. If you are looking at inspiration photos, ask your photographer whether they have shot the new site yet.

## How the reservation works

Sapphire Point is reserved on Recreation.gov as a day use venue. The Forest Service and Recreation.gov both describe it as reservable in **three hour blocks** for private gatherings. The Recreation.gov listing shows a price range of $120 to $142 for a reservation; the exact amount appears when you choose a date and block. The booking window opens on a schedule shown on the listing, and summer and fall weekend sunrise and sunset blocks go early, so set a reminder.

Your confirmation, printed or on a phone, must be with someone at the site during your block, and it goes on the dashboard of the two vehicles in your dedicated spaces.

A reservation is **not exclusive**. The trail and overlooks stay open to the public the whole time, and hikers will walk past your ceremony. Plan your photos with that in mind.

## Group size

Recreation.gov limits reserved events to no more than 30 people. The Forest Service page words it as a maximum of 30 people on site for reservation holders, including the two assigned parking spaces. People without a reservation are limited to groups of ten, which is worth knowing if you hoped to skip the reservation for a tiny ceremony: with ten or fewer people and no setup you are simply visitors, but you have no claim on the overlook if a reserved event is there.

## Parking: two spaces

Reserved events get two dedicated parking spaces, each for a vehicle up to 20 feet long. No other parking is authorized for your event or your guests. If you need more than two cars' worth of people, the rules allow one multi passenger vehicle, coordinated by you, to drop off and pick up guests in the loading zone; it may not park. In practice that means a van or small shuttle from Frisco, Keystone or Breckenridge for any group bigger than about eight.

## What you can and cannot bring

The Recreation.gov listing sets these event guidelines:

- Decorations must be free standing and natural, and removed right after the event.
- No chairs. Bringing in chairs is not allowed.
- No stakes, poles or anything that disturbs the ground.
- No event tents.
- No food or beverages.
- No open flames, including candles.
- Nothing released or scattered: no seeds, petals, rice or animals.
- Acoustic, unamplified music only, quiet enough that it cannot be heard more than 150 feet away.

A bouquet, a handheld arrangement and a guitar are fine. An arch, a champagne toast and a speaker are not.

The listing also says commercial use at Sapphire Point is prohibited and directs questions to the Dillon Ranger District at 970-468-5400. If you are hiring a photographer, planner or officiant, ask them how they work at Sapphire Point and confirm with the district if anything is unclear. We could not find a published explanation of how this applies to vendors, so we will not guess.

## Light and timing

The view runs north and west across the reservoir toward the Gore and Tenmile ranges. Sunrise brings soft light and alpenglow on the peaks across the water, and the emptiest trail. Sunset is the busiest time and puts the sun behind the mountains, which is lovely for silhouettes and harder for faces. In July and August, afternoon thunderstorms are routine above 9,000 feet, so a morning block is the safer bet. Late September brings yellow aspen on the hillsides below. Winter ceremonies are possible and beautiful, with a frozen white reservoir, if your guests can handle cold and ice.

## The license

The marriage license comes from any Colorado county clerk. The Summit County clerk in Breckenridge requires appointments for non residents, with four slots a day; our [Summit County license page](/resources/summit-county-marriage-license) explains. Many couples pick up a license in [Golden](/resources/jefferson-county-marriage-license) on the drive up instead. You can [solemnize the marriage yourselves](/resources/self-solemnization-colorado) right at the overlook.

## A simple plan

Book a three hour morning block, reserve a van for anyone beyond the two cars, keep the group under 30 including your photographer and officiant, bring only what you can carry, and plan breakfast or brunch in Frisco afterward. If you want chairs, a toast or a bigger group, hold the ceremony at a private venue instead; our [micro wedding venues list](/venues/micro-wedding-venues-colorado) includes Breckenridge options. The [Summit County guide](/guides/summit-county) covers Loveland Pass and the resort decks, and [Summit County vendors](/region/summit-county) know this overlook well.
