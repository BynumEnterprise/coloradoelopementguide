Rocky Mountain National Park publishes exactly how many weddings it will permit: 60 ceremonies a month from May through October, 40 a month from November through April, no more than two a day at any one site and six a day park wide. Applications open one year ahead of the wedding month and are reviewed in the order received. On November 5, 2026 the park's wedding page still said all dates for May, June, August, September and October 2026 were fully booked. If your month is gone, this page is the plan B. Every fee and rule below comes from the office that sets it.

## First, confirm the month is really full

The park has no online calendar. You email a completed application to romo_fees_permits@nps.gov with a specific date, site and two hour window and wait the 7 to 10 days the park quotes. Three things to try before you give up:

1. Ask for a different site. The two per site per day limit means a full Sprague Lake morning does not mean a full park; the other twelve sites each have their own count.
2. Ask for a weekday or an afternoon window. Couples cluster on Saturday sunrises.
3. Ask about the west side. Harbison Meadows and Timber Creek Campground Amphitheater near Grand Lake count against the same cap but are requested far less often; our [Grand Lake guide](/resources/grand-lake-elopement) covers both.

If the Commercial Services Office (970 586 1210) still says no, the park is closed to you for that month. A ceremony anywhere other than a designated site is a violation the park says it will cite against the couple, the photographer and the officiant. Portraits after a ceremony held elsewhere need no permit; that is how most couples below use the park.

## Hermit Park Open Space, Larimer County

Hermit Park is a 1,362 acre county open space two miles southeast of town on US 36, between 7,880 and 8,964 feet, with ponderosa forest, a 2.5 acre meadow and a view of Longs Peak. Larimer County markets it as its Estes Park wedding site. You reserve the group pavilion and use the meadow beside it: stone columns, 20 picnic tables, a gas fire pit, a catering area with grills, generator power on request and vault toilets about 150 feet away.

The numbers, from the county's pavilion page: $500 for the day, 150 people, 10 a.m. to 8 p.m., reservable up to 24 months ahead at larimercamping.com or 970 577 2090. Every vehicle also needs a $10 daily entrance permit. Event tents and your own chairs are allowed, acoustic music is preferred, alcohol is not allowed at pavilions, one food truck may park there, and petals and bubbles are fine for an exit while fake petals, seeds and rice are not. Leashed dogs are welcome.

Two constraints. The property is open March 1 through December 20, so January and February dates go elsewhere. And the pavilion books well ahead, so a couple that lost a September park date should check Hermit Park the same day. Camper cabins for guests run $110 to $165 a night from May through October.

## Roosevelt National Forest along the Peak to Peak

The Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests wrap around the park on the east and south, and their rules are the most permissive near Estes Park. The forest's events page sets the test: a gathering needs a permit if it is 75 or more people, if it is commercial, if it uses props of any kind (arches, chairs, tables, tents, lighting), or if it is in a wilderness or the Arapaho National Recreation Area. A ceremony with no props on an existing trail or pullout needs nothing. Weddings are not allowed along Highway 5 or at developed sites on Mount Blue Sky. A permit, when needed, takes six months to a year.

Practical spots are along Colorado 7 south of Lily Lake toward Allenspark and on the Peak to Peak Highway, where pullouts face Longs Peak and Mount Meeker. Your photographer may need the forest's photography authorization, so ask whether they hold one. Ranger district contacts are in our [national forest and BLM guide](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements).

## Town of Estes Park parks and venues

The Town of Estes Park rents its spaces through the Estes Park Events Complex (events@estes.org, 970 577 3900). Mrs. Walsh's Garden, a fenced botanic garden at the west end of Elkhorn Avenue, is the one the town describes as a wedding ceremony setting: a reception area inside the gate, a stream side path, benches, and parking and restrooms across the street at Performance Park. It is open Memorial Day through September 30 with no winter rentals. George Hix Riverside Plaza, Performance Park and the O'Connor Pavilion are also rentable. The town does not publish rental prices; you submit its venue request form or event permit application and it quotes from there, so allow time for that exchange.

Lake Estes belongs to the Estes Valley Recreation and Park District rather than the town; we could not verify a published event fee there, so ask the district directly.

## YMCA of the Rockies, Estes Park Center

The Estes Park Center on Tunnel Road sits at the edge of the park with a chapel, indoor halls, outdoor ceremony sites and lodging for guests. The YMCA's wedding page puts venue rental at roughly $1,000 to $6,000 depending on location, season and guest count, ceremony only or with reception, for groups up to 400. It is private property, so the park's rules on arches, chairs and decorations do not apply, and it is open every month. Group inquiries go to 800 777 9622.

## Private venues and the west side

Estes Park has a deep list of lodges, ranches and small venues that host a ceremony and reception together; the [Estes Park venue list](/venues/estes-park-wedding-venues) shows capacities and starting prices where vendors publish them. Across Trail Ridge Road, Grand Lake has the park's two west side sites, rentable town parks and Arapaho National Recreation Area shoreline. The drive is about two hours while Trail Ridge is open, usually late May to mid October, and three and a half hours around the park when it is closed.

| Month | RMNP status | Best alternative | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January and February | 40 permit cap, usually open | RMNP itself, then YMCA or a private venue | Hermit Park is closed until March 1; town parks are closed for winter |
| March and April | 40 cap, mud season | Hermit Park opens March 1; YMCA | Snow above 9,000 feet, pavilion dates are easy to get |
| May | 60 cap, 2026 was full | Hermit Park meadow; national forest pullout | Trail Ridge Road usually opens the last week of May |
| June | 60 cap, 2026 was full | Hermit Park; Mrs. Walsh's Garden from Memorial Day | Afternoon storms start, go early |
| July | 60 cap, not listed as full for 2026 | Try the park first, west side second | Peak crowds, timed entry in effect |
| August | 60 cap, 2026 was full | Hermit Park; national forest; YMCA | Wildflowers still up high, storms most afternoons |
| September | 60 cap, fills first | Hermit Park 24 months out; forest pullouts on Colorado 7 | Aspens turn late in the month, elk rut closes park meadows 5 p.m. to 10 a.m. |
| October | 60 cap, fills first | Hermit Park through December 20; YMCA | Trail Ridge closed for the season on October 18 in 2024 and November 14 in 2025 |
| November and December | 40 cap, usually open | RMNP itself, then Hermit Park to December 20 | Town garden closed after September 30 |

In the months the park is realistically full, Hermit Park is the public land answer for a group, the national forest is the answer for two people and no props, and YMCA or a private venue is the answer for chairs, an arch and dinner in one place. In the months the park has room, apply to the park and hold the others as backups.

## Common questions

### Can we get married at an overlook in the park if we keep it to the two of us?

No. The park requires a permit for two or more people, allows ceremonies only at its thirteen designated sites, and cites ceremonies anywhere else. Marry outside the park, then drive in for portraits with a normal entrance pass.

### Does a Hermit Park reservation get us into the park afterward?

No. The $10 Hermit Park vehicle permit covers only Hermit Park. The national park charges its own $30 one day or $35 seven day vehicle pass, and in summer you may also need a timed entry reservation.

### Do we need a permit for a national forest ceremony with ten guests and a bouquet?

Not under the forest's published test, as long as you are under 75 people, outside wilderness and the Arapaho National Recreation Area, and bring no props. A bouquet is not a prop; chairs, an arch and a table are.

### Is the Hermit Park pavilion open in winter?

No. The property is open March 1 through December 20, weather permitting. For January and February the park's 40 permit cap is usually the easier door.
