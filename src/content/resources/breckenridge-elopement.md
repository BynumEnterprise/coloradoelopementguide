Breckenridge gives you two weddings in one place: a Victorian Main Street with restaurants and lodging for every guest, and national forest starting at the edge of town. Most couples who elope here do the paperwork in an afternoon and spend the rest of their planning on one question: who controls the spot you want to stand on. In and around Breck that answer is the Town, the Forest Service, or a ski resort, and each runs things differently.

## Your license

Breckenridge is the Summit County seat, so the county clerk is right in town. The catch is that Summit County requires appointments for non residents and asks Colorado residents to use their home county. Our [Summit County marriage license guide](/resources/summit-county-marriage-license) walks through the slots and the fee. Any Colorado county's license works here, valid for 35 days, with no witnesses required, and you can [solemnize it yourselves](/resources/self-solemnization-colorado).

## Town of Breckenridge rules

The Town requires a special event permit for planned events on Town owned property, and its smallest category fits a wedding closely. A Tier Zero event, as the Town defines it, is:

- 1 to 150 people at any one time,
- on one day, on Town owned property,
- private, meaning the public is not invited, and
- low impact, with no public safety staffing beyond normal operations.

Applications are due 90 to 365 days before the event. Late applications are accepted but carry a higher fee and possibly extra requirements, and the application must come with a Town business license or proof of one. The Town does not say whether a couple and a photographer in a park counts as an event, so if you want a ceremony on Town land with chairs, a few guests or a set start time, call the Special Event Permit Administrator at the Breckenridge Tourism Office, (970) 453-5048, and ask before you book anything else. A private reception indoors on private property is outside the special event rules entirely.

## White River National Forest

Most of the mountain scenery around town, including Boreas Pass and the forest along Hoosier Pass, is the White River National Forest, managed locally by the Dillon Ranger District at 680 Blue River Parkway in Silverthorne, (970) 468-5400, open weekdays 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Federal rules treat a gathering of 75 or more people as noncommercial group use that needs authorization; a small elopement falls well below that line. Our [national forest and BLM guide](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements) covers how the rule works and what changes if a fee is charged. If your plan includes an arch, chairs or a group close to the threshold, ask the district first.

## Where couples go

| Spot | Who manages it | What to know |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| Boreas Pass Road | County road through national forest | Gate closes for winter; summit tops 11,000 feet |
| Hoosier Pass | National forest along CO 9 | On the Continental Divide south of town, about 11,500 feet |
| Sapphire Point | Forest Service, reserved on Recreation.gov | 30 people at most |
| Loveland Pass | Forest Service along US 6 | High, exposed and windy on the Divide |
| Town parks and open space | Town of Breckenridge | Ask about the Tier Zero permit |
| Ten Mile Station | Breckenridge Ski Resort | Booked through the resort |

**Boreas Pass** follows an old railroad grade east of town into aspen and long views of the Tenmile Range. The Forest Service notes that Boreas Pass Road is closed seasonally by Summit County Open Space and Road and Bridge, with spring opening dependent on snow and county plowing, so a winter ceremony here means skis or snowshoes past the gate. **Hoosier Pass** is the paved option, right on the highway to Alma, and is often windy and cold even in summer.

**Sapphire Point** above Dillon Reservoir is the best known ceremony site in the county that you can formally reserve; see the [Sapphire Point guide](/resources/sapphire-point-overlook-elopement). **Loveland Pass** is the high alpine choice on the Continental Divide; the [Loveland Pass guide](/resources/loveland-pass-elopement) covers the road and the avalanche season.

## Breckenridge Ski Resort weddings

Breckenridge Ski Resort lists four wedding spaces and says it handles groups from 2 to more than 300. Published figures from the resort:

- **Ten Mile Station**, on mountain inside the national forest: June to October, up to 200 guests, food and beverage minimum from $27,200, venue package $12,000 including mountain shuttle and ceremony chairs.
- **The Maggie**, by Maggie Pond near Main Street: May to October, up to 200 guests, minimum from $10,200, package from $3,000.
- **Elevation Ballroom**, a short walk from Main Street: May to October with some winter dates, up to 200 guests, minimum from $8,000, package $5,000.

The resort offers a complimentary wedding night stay to new bookings with a block of 10 or more rooms. Final pricing varies by event, so treat these as starting points.

## Keystone and Copper Mountain

Two more resorts sit within half an hour of Breckenridge (estimate), and both run their own wedding teams.

**Keystone** publishes four venues. Timber Ridge sits at the top of North Peak, reached by gondola from River Run Village, with ceremonies on its deck or lawn facing the Gore and Tenmile ranges: June to September, up to 300 guests, food and beverage minimum from $27,500, package from $10,000. The Ranch, a 1930s homestead on the golf course, takes up to 120 from mid May through October, minimum from $17,500 and package $5,000. Ski Tip Lodge, an old stagecoach stop that became a ski lodge in 1947, takes up to 100 with nine B&B rooms on site, minimum from $9,800. Kindred Resort at the base operates year round, with a ballroom for 200 and a courtyard for 160 and minimums from $5,000. Keystone offers the same 10 room block wedding night stay as Breckenridge.

**Copper Mountain** groups its ceremony sites by village. In Center Village you can marry at the Aerie, reached by the American Eagle lift, or in the Copper Mountain Chapel. East Village has Copper Creek Vista and the indoor Mezzanine, with receptions in the Grand Hall or JJ's Rocky Mountain Tavern. West Village has Koko's Hut, reached by the Kokomo Express lift. Copper does not publish prices online; ask its wedding team for a quote.

## Altitude and seasons

Breckenridge sits at about 9,600 feet, and Boreas and Hoosier passes are well above 11,000. Guests flying in from sea level feel it on the first day. Build in a rest day, keep water and sunscreen at the ceremony, and keep the hike short if older relatives are coming.

**Summer** brings green slopes, open roads and afternoon thunderstorms; plan ceremonies for the morning above treeline. **Fall** turns the aspens on Boreas Pass gold, usually in late September; our [fall elopement guide](/resources/colorado-fall-elopement) covers the timing. **Winter** closes the dirt roads but opens the resorts' snowy settings; see the [winter elopement guide](/resources/winter-elopement-colorado). **Spring** is mud season, with many roads and resort venues still closed.
