Colorado's more than 40 state parks fill a gap between the national park, where every ceremony needs a $300 permit, and the national forest, where a small ceremony needs nothing. Colorado Parks and Wildlife runs weddings through a document called a Special Activity Agreement, handled park by park, and offers reservable amphitheaters and overlooks at several parks for a fee. The process is less formal than Rocky Mountain National Park and more structured than a trailhead. This page explains how it works and what to expect at the five parks couples ask about most.

## The Special Activity Agreement

CPW's statewide weddings page says a Special Activity Agreement or photography permit may be required to hold an event or group activity in a park, and tells you to check the specific park's page for whether one is needed and how to apply. In practice most parks treat any wedding, including an elopement, as an event that needs the agreement. Cheyenne Mountain State Park, for example, states that a Special Activity Agreement is required for all weddings and must be filled out, sent in and approved by the park manager before your visit.

The agreement itself is a short form. You describe the date, time, location in the park, number of people and any equipment, and you agree to a set of conditions: you are responsible for damage, vehicles stay in designated areas, every vehicle displays a valid park pass, alcohol over 3.2 percent is prohibited without a state permit, and you hold the state harmless. Some parks ask for proof of liability insurance for larger events. Deadlines differ. Published packets from individual parks ask for 45 days in one case and 90 days in another, so contact your park as soon as you have a date.

There is no statewide wedding fee. What you pay depends on the park: nothing beyond entry for a small ceremony at an unreserved spot, or a facility fee for an amphitheater, overlook or group picnic area. Those fees are set by each park and paid when you reserve.

## Park passes

Every vehicle entering a state park needs a pass. Daily vehicle passes run $10 to $15 at Eldorado Canyon, Staunton, Rifle Falls and Ridgway, and $12 to $17 at Golden Gate Canyon, with the price varying by season and by whether you buy online. An individual walk in or bike in pass is $4. Colorado residents who registered a vehicle since 2023 may already hold a Keep Colorado Wild pass. For a ceremony with ten carloads of guests, passes are a real line item; the [cost guide](/resources/colorado-elopement-cost) accounts for them.

## Photography permits

CPW notes that wedding photos taken by a professional photographer may require a photography permit, again at the park's discretion. Some parks publish a separate commercial photography agreement. Ask the park when you file your Special Activity Agreement and tell your photographer what they said. Photographers in our [directory](/category/photographers) who shoot state parks regularly often hold annual permits.

## Reservable ceremony sites

CPW's statewide page lists the sites built for ceremonies:

- **Staunton State Park** amphitheater: a rustic outdoor setting with an arch for ceremonies and bench seating for up to 60 guests. Weddings are welcome.
- **Ridgway State Park** visitor center overlook: a backdrop of the San Juan Mountains and Ridgway Reservoir, accommodating up to 100 guests.
- **Castlewood Canyon** Bridge Canyon Overlook: a deck overlooking the canyon for up to 60 guests, bring your own chairs.
- **Roxborough** Fountain Valley and Lyons overlooks: up to 25 guests each.
- **Cheyenne Mountain** Prairie Falcon amphitheater: rock step seating for up to 160.
- **Castlewood Canyon** and **Cherry Creek** amphitheaters: up to 100 each.

Group picnic areas at most parks serve as reception sites, typically with a shade shelter, grill and tables; the group picnic reservation is separate from the ceremony site.

## The parks couples pick

**Eldorado Canyon**, south of Boulder. Sandstone walls, a creek, and climbers overhead. The park requires a timed entry reservation to drive in on Saturdays, Sundays and summer holidays from May 1 through October 1; weekday mornings do not. The inner canyon is small and busy, so plan a weekday or a shoulder season date and file the Special Activity Agreement early. Vendors are in our [Boulder region](/region/boulder) and the [Boulder guide](/guides/boulder).

**Golden Gate Canyon**, northwest of Golden. Aspen groves, meadows and the Panorama Point overlook of the Continental Divide. The park has cabins and yurts for a wedding night, which CPW specifically pitches as a honeymoon option. Panorama Point is a popular ceremony spot; ask the park whether it needs to be reserved for your date. See the [Denver and Foothills guide](/guides/denver-foothills).

**Staunton**, near Conifer. The reservable amphitheater with its arch is the most turnkey ceremony site in the state park system for a group up to 60, and the park's granite domes and meadows make for strong portraits. Reserve the amphitheater through the park and file the agreement at the same time.

**Rifle Falls**, north of Rifle. A triple waterfall with limestone caves, in a small park that is often quiet. Good for a couple alone or a handful of guests; the park has no large reservable ceremony site, so the Special Activity Agreement covers a simple ceremony near the falls. It is about three hours from Denver and an hour from Glenwood Springs.

**Ridgway**, between Ridgway and Montrose. The visitor center overlook faces the Sneffels Range and holds up to 100 guests, and the park has yurts. It is the practical choice for a Telluride or Ouray wedding that wants a reservable site with parking; see the [Telluride and San Juans guide](/guides/telluride-san-juans).

## How the day usually runs

1. Pick the park and the spot, then call or email the park office with your date, headcount and plans.
2. Submit the Special Activity Agreement and any facility reservation; pay the facility fee if there is one.
3. Confirm whether your photographer needs a permit.
4. Buy park passes for every vehicle, or send guests the online link.
5. Bring your signed agreement, your marriage license and a pen.

The license is a separate matter from the park: any Colorado county clerk issues it for $30, no witnesses or officiant required. Our [marriage license page](/resources/colorado-marriage-license) has the details. If you are deciding between a state park and a national forest site, the [national forest page](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements) lays out the tradeoffs.
