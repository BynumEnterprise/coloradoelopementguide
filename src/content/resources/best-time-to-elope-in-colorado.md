There is no single best month to elope in Colorado. There is a best month for what you want: wildflowers, gold aspens, snow, empty trails, or a road to 12,000 feet. The mountains run on their own calendar, and it is set by three things you cannot move: when the high roads open, when the afternoon storms arrive, and when the snow comes back. Pick the setting first, then the month.

## Colorado month by month

The table is for the high country, roughly 8,000 to 12,000 feet. Denver and the foothills run several weeks ahead in spring and behind in fall.

| Month | Mountain conditions | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | Deep snow, cold, short days | Snowy ceremonies, ski towns | High passes closed; traction law |
| February | Deep snow, more sun | Ski town elopements, lodge venues | Holiday weekend crowds |
| March | Heaviest snow in many areas | Ski season ceremonies | Spring storms, road closures |
| April | Snow up high, melt below | Quiet dates, lower valleys | Mud season; resorts close |
| May | Melting, muddy trails | Desert and foothills; late month high roads | Trail Ridge and Independence Pass open late May |
| June | Green, long days, snow lingers up high | Lakes, valleys, longest daylight | Snow on high trails; first storms |
| July | Warmest, peak wildflowers | Alpine meadows, high roads | Afternoon thunderstorms most days |
| August | Warm, flowers fading late | High lakes, early morning hikes | Afternoon thunderstorms; smoke some years |
| September | Cool, clear, aspens turning | Fall color, fewer storms | Weekend crowds; early snow up high |
| October | Cold mornings, first snow | Foothill aspens, quiet trails | Trail Ridge normally closes mid October |
| November | Early winter | Cheap dates, early ski openings | Kebler, Cottonwood and Independence Pass closed |
| December | Snow, holiday lights | Winter ceremonies, ski towns | Shortest days; holiday pricing |

## When the high roads open

Many of the views couples see in photos sit on roads that close for half the year.

- **Trail Ridge Road** in Rocky Mountain National Park normally closes in mid October and opens to through travel during the last week of May, weather permitting, according to the Park Service. In 2025 it opened May 30 and closed for winter on Nov. 14. The recorded status line is (970) 586-1222.
- **Independence Pass** on CO 82 closes around Nov. 7 and typically opens the Thursday before Memorial Day, per CDOT.
- **Kebler Pass** and **Cottonwood Pass**, both maintained by Gunnison County, are typically closed November to May, per CDOT.

If your plan depends on one of these roads, a date from late June through September is the safe window. Late May and October are possible but can change with one storm. In Rocky Mountain National Park you also need a timed entry reservation from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. during the summer season; in 2026 that ran May 22 through Oct. 12. ## Wildflowers, aspens and snow

**Wildflowers** peak in July in most of the high country, running into early August at the highest meadows. This is also the busiest month on trails and roads.

**Aspens** follow elevation. CDOT's 2026 fall release, using Colorado State Forest Service estimates, put the northern mountains and high passes above 9,500 feet in early to mid September, the central mountains in mid to late September, the San Juans in late September, and the foothills below 8,000 feet in late September to early October. Peak can move a week or more with drought. Our [Colorado fall elopement guide](/resources/colorado-fall-elopement) covers how to commit to a date.

**Snow** can fall in any month above treeline. Real winter usually arrives in November and stays into April or May up high. If you want snow on purpose, read our [winter elopement guide](/resources/winter-elopement-colorado), which covers road closures, the traction law and winter park sites.

**Mud season** is the gap between ski season and summer, roughly April into early June, and again briefly in late fall. Trails are wet, some resort businesses close, and the high roads are still shut. It is the cheapest and quietest time to marry in the mountains if you stay in the valleys.

## Storms and lightning

Summer afternoons in the Colorado mountains often bring thunderstorms, especially in July and August. Rocky Mountain National Park says most thunderstorm activity happens after noon and advises starting high country outings early. On open tundra, you are likely the tallest thing around.

The National Weather Service says the only completely safe action in a thunderstorm is to get inside a safe building or vehicle, and to wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back out. For an elopement, that means two practical rules:

- **Hold summer ceremonies above treeline at sunrise or in the morning.** Sunset at high elevation in July is a gamble.
- **Park close to your ceremony spot** so the car is your shelter, and build a buffer into your timeline.

## Weekday or weekend, sunrise or sunset

**Weekdays win.** Trailheads, overlooks and permit calendars are all less crowded Tuesday through Thursday. CDOT specifically recommends midweek mountain trips during fall color season. Vendors are also more flexible on weekdays.

**Sunrise** gives you calm air, soft light, empty parking lots and clear skies before the storms build. The tradeoff is a very early start.

**Sunset** is gentler on your schedule and works well in fall, winter and in the lower valleys. In winter it comes early; in Denver it is before 5 p.m. in late December.

## How far ahead to book

These are typical lead times, not rules, and popular dates move faster.

| What | Typical lead time (estimate) |
|---|---|
| Peak season photographer (July, September weekends) | 9 to 12 months |
| Off season photographer | 3 to 6 months |
| National park or forest permit | Apply as soon as the window opens |
| Lodging in ski towns or fall color areas | 6 to 12 months |
| Officiant | 2 to 6 months |
