Loveland Pass is one of the easiest places in Colorado to stand on the Continental Divide without a long hike. You park, walk a few minutes up the ridge, and the peaks of Summit County and Clear Creek County fall away on both sides. For a small ceremony there is no reservation and no fee. The price you pay is weather, wind and a highway that answers to winter, so this guide is mostly about planning around those.

## Where it is

Loveland Pass carries US 6 over the Divide between Loveland Ski Area on the east end and Arapahoe Basin on the west, according to the Forest Service. Driving west on Interstate 70, you exit onto US 6 just before the Eisenhower Johnson Memorial Tunnel and climb the switchbacks to the summit lot. CDOT notes the tunnel saves 9.1 miles over the pass, which is why most traffic never sees it.

USGS elevation data puts the summit trailhead at about 11,990 feet. The Forest Service's trailhead listing gives 12,500 feet, so use 11,990 for the road and expect the ridge trails to climb from there.

## Who manages the land

Two ranger districts meet near the pass:

- **East side (Clear Creek County):** The Forest Service files the Loveland Pass Trailhead under the Clear Creek Ranger District of the Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests. The office is in Idaho Springs, open by appointment, at 970-295-6600.
- **West side (Summit County):** National forest land in Summit County is handled by the Dillon Ranger District of the White River National Forest, 680 Blue River Parkway in Silverthorne, 970-468-5400.

The boundary is not marked at the lot. If your plans go beyond a few people standing on the ridge, call Clear Creek first and ask.

## Do you need a permit?

For most elopements, no. The Arapaho and Roosevelt events page says a permit is required when any of these apply:

- **Group use:** 75 or more people, counting participants and spectators. This comes from the federal definition in 36 CFR 251.51.
- **Commercial use:** charging a fee or selling something. Your wedding is not commercial, though your photographer's business may need its own permit.
- **Props:** the forest states that events with props of any kind require a permit. Chairs, an arch or a table count.
- **Protected areas:** Wilderness and other designated areas may need a permit at any size.

Two people, a photographer and an officiant on the ridge with a bouquet is ordinary use. Our [national forest elopement guide](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements) explains the full framework. If you want a site you can actually reserve, [Sapphire Point](/resources/sapphire-point-overlook-elopement) above Dillon Reservoir is the Summit County option.

## The road: closures and hazmat trucks

Two CDOT facts shape a Loveland Pass morning.

**Tanker trucks use the pass.** Hazardous materials trucks, such as gas tankers, are not allowed through the Eisenhower Johnson tunnel and are routed over Loveland Pass on US 6. Only when the pass closes does CDOT escort them through the tunnel. Expect big trucks on tight switchbacks at any hour. Park fully inside the lot and never stand on the pavement for a photo.

**The pass closes.** CDOT runs remote avalanche control systems above US 6 across from Arapahoe Basin, and storms bring safety closures while crews work. Closures are not only a winter problem: in June 2025 a mountain slide shut the pass for an extended cleanup. Check COtrip.org the night before and again before you leave, and keep a backup site in mind.

## Avalanche terrain

Whenever there is snow on the ground, the slopes around the pass are avalanche terrain. The Forest Service notes the pass is popular with backcountry skiers, and CDOT controls slide paths above the road. For a winter or spring ceremony, stay in or right beside the plowed lot, do not walk out onto snow slopes for a better angle, and read the Colorado Avalanche Information Center forecast at avalanche.state.co.us before you go. If you want to be up on the ridge in snow season, hire a photographer with avalanche training.

## Weather, wind and altitude

Wind is the constant at Loveland Pass. Even on a clear summer morning it can be strong enough to take a veil, so pin everything and skip anything that can blow away. In July and August, thunderstorms often build over the Divide by afternoon. You are above treeline with nowhere to hide, so plan a morning ceremony and head for the cars at the first thunder.

Guests coming from sea level feel 11,990 feet. Have them drink water the day before, take the short walk slowly and skip the ridge climb if they are not well acclimated.

### What to wear

Layers, even in July. A warm coat over a dress for the walk, gloves, and shoes with real grip for gravel, ice or snow. Sunglasses and sunscreen matter more up here than in town. In winter, plan for cold most Denver guests have never stood in.

## Sunrise logistics

Sunrise is the best time to marry here: light on the peaks, calmer air, fewer cars and the full morning before storms. Stay in Georgetown, Silverthorne, Dillon or Keystone so the drive is short. Arrive 45 minutes before sunrise, carpool to keep the cars few, and scout the lot in daylight the day before if you can. Bring a headlamp for the walk up and a thermos for after.

## Leave No Trace on alpine tundra

The plants above treeline grow slowly and do not recover quickly from trampling. Stay on rock, gravel and existing trail. Use no petals, confetti or rice, and pack out every ribbon and cork. There are no restrooms at the summit trailhead, so plan a stop before the climb. Dogs must be on a hand held leash.

## Your marriage license

Any Colorado county license works on the pass. The two nearest clerks:

| Office | Location | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Summit County Clerk and Recorder | Breckenridge | Non residents need an appointment: 9 a.m., 11 a.m., 2 p.m. or 3 p.m. weekdays; $39 with three certified copies |
| Clear Creek County Clerk and Recorder | 405 Argentine Street, Georgetown | $30, both of you present or an absentee affidavit; the directory lists Monday to Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., so call 303-679-2339 first |

A license is good for 35 days, and the certificate goes back within 63 days of the ceremony. Our [Summit County license guide](/resources/summit-county-marriage-license) has the details, and you can [solemnize the marriage yourselves](/resources/self-solemnization-colorado) at the summit.

## Common questions

### Do you need a permit to elope at Loveland Pass?
Not for a small, noncommercial ceremony without props. A group of 75 or more, or chairs and an arch, means calling the ranger district about a special use permit.

### Is Loveland Pass open in winter?
It is the hazmat truck route over the Divide, so it is normally open, but it closes during storms and avalanche work. Check COtrip.org before you drive.

### How many guests can come?
There is no posted cap below the 75 person group use line, but the lot and ridge are small and shared. Keep it to a handful of cars.

### Can we hike higher for photos?
The ridge trails climb from the lot. In summer, go early. In snow season, stay off the slopes unless someone in your group knows avalanche terrain.
