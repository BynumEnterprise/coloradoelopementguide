Gold aspen against dark spruce and the first snow on the peaks: that is the picture most couples have in mind when they think of a Colorado elopement. The season that produces it is short, it moves with elevation, and it brings the biggest crowds of the year to the places you most want to stand. Plan it like a weather event, not a date on the calendar.

## When do the aspens change in Colorado?

The Colorado State Forest Service puts it simply: in general, September and early October are when the higher and more northern aspen stands show color, and lower, more southerly places follow. Aspens take their cue from shorter days, but tree health, drought or extra moisture, elevation and latitude all shift the timing, so a spot that was perfect one year can disappoint the next.

CDOT's fall travel release for 2026, drawing on the Forest Service, gave these regional estimates:

| Area | Elevation | Estimated peak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern mountains and high passes | Above 9,500 feet | Early to mid September |
| Central mountains and aspen corridors | 8,000 to 9,500 feet | Mid to late September |
| Southern Colorado and the San Juans | Varied | Late September |
| Foothills and Front Range | Below 8,000 feet | Late September to early October |

That year's estimates ran early because of drought stress, so treat the table as a pattern, not a promise. The practical rule: the color moves downhill over a few weeks. If you can only pick one date, aim for the last ten days of September in the central mountains and stay flexible on location. If your photographer suggests moving the ceremony up or down a thousand feet a week out, listen.

## The famous spots and their fall rules

### Maroon Bells

The most photographed fall view in the state, and the most managed. Reservations are required to visit the Maroon Bells Scenic Area. In 2026 the area was closed to inbound private vehicles from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. between May 22 and Oct. 18; in that window you arrive by the reserved shuttle from Aspen Highlands or hold a parking reservation and arrive before 8 a.m. or after 5 p.m. Maroon Creek Road stays open to cars until early November.

For a ceremony, the amphitheater above Maroon Lake is reserved separately on Recreation.gov, and in recent seasons it has been unavailable on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays in September. Our [Maroon Bells elopement permit guide](/resources/maroon-bells-elopement-permit) covers the amphitheater, the vehicle passes and the blackout days in detail. A weekday sunrise is both easier to book and far quieter.

### Rocky Mountain National Park

Fall is the elk rut. The Park Service describes elk coming down to the montane meadows every autumn to breed, with bulls bugling through the evenings. To protect the herds, areas in Horseshoe Park, Moraine Park, Upper Beaver Meadows, Harbison Meadow and Holzwarth Meadow are closed to off trail foot travel from 5 p.m. to 10 a.m. daily during the mating season. Upper Beaver Meadows and Harbison Meadows are both designated wedding sites, so ask the park how the closure affects your time slot and stay on the trail.

Timed entry is still running in early fall. In 2026 a reservation was needed from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. through Oct. 12, and the Bear Lake Road corridor needed one from 5 a.m. to 6 p.m. through Oct. 18. Your wedding permit counts as your reservation, but guests arriving in separate cars should check. The park's summer group limits run through the second Monday in October, and August through October 2026 filled completely, so apply on the first day your month opens a year ahead. The [RMNP wedding permit guide](/resources/rocky-mountain-national-park-wedding-permit) explains how, and the [Estes Park guide](/guides/estes-park) covers where to stay.

### Crested Butte, Ouray and Telluride

The southern and western mountains turn a little later and draw fewer day trippers from Denver. [Kebler Pass](https://www.codot.gov/travel/coloradobyways/southwest/west-elk-loop), west of Crested Butte, is a classic fall drive on a 31 mile gravel section of the West Elk Loop that CDOT says is typically closed from November to May. [Last Dollar Road](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/gmug/recreation/last-dollar-road-638) between Ridgway and Telluride is, in the Forest Service's words, a popular road for viewing fall colors; it can become impassable when wet, and a high clearance four wheel drive vehicle is recommended. See our [Crested Butte elopement guide](/resources/crested-butte-elopement) and [Ouray elopement guide](/resources/ouray-elopement).

### Closer to Denver

- **Kenosha Pass** on U.S. 285. The Forest Service says the area is well known for its fall aspens and that the trailhead gets very busy during peak leaf season, late September to early October.
- **[Guanella Pass](https://www.codot.gov/travel/coloradobyways/north-central/guanella-pass)** between Georgetown and Grant. CDOT notes strict parking restrictions at the summit, extending at least half a mile north and south, during peak leaf season.
- **[Boreas Pass](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/recreation/boreas-pass-road-th-10)** from Breckenridge to Como, on an old railroad grade that the Forest Service calls a great road for fall colors. It is open to highway legal vehicles from May to October and generally closed to motorized vehicles from October to May at Summit County's discretion.

All three are national forest settings where a small, simple ceremony usually needs no permit; read [national forest and BLM elopements](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements) first.

## Early snow and cold mornings

Fall in the high country is a shoulder season. CDOT warns that high elevation passes can see early autumn snowstorms, and the state's traction law took effect on Sept. 1 along key corridors including the I-70 mountain corridor: winter ready tires with at least 3/16 inch of tread, or approved traction devices. Expect a late September sunrise at 10,000 feet to feel like winter, even after a warm afternoon.

Pack for both seasons: a warm layer for each of you, a blanket for between shots, and shoes that can handle frost or a dusting of snow. Have a lower backup location in mind if a storm closes a pass.

## Crowds and timing

Peak weekends are the busiest days of the year at Kenosha, Guanella and the Maroon Bells. CDOT's advice for leaf season is to travel midweek, Tuesday through Thursday, to avoid extreme weekend congestion. For an elopement that means:

- **Choose a weekday** if you possibly can.
- **Go at sunrise.** The light on yellow aspens is softer and the overlooks are nearly empty.
- **Never stop in the road for photos.** CDOT asks drivers to use designated turnouts and paved parking areas only.

Days are still long enough for a relaxed schedule. Our [Colorado elopement timeline](/resources/colorado-elopement-timeline) has a sample fall day, and [best places to elope in Colorado](/resources/best-places-to-elope-in-colorado) lists the permit status of each spot.
