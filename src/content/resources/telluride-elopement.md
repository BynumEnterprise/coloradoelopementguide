Our [Telluride and the San Juans guide](/guides/telluride-san-juans) is about the place: why couples make the drive, which overlooks are worth it, how to time the aspens. This page is the paperwork and the road conditions underneath that guide. It answers the questions that come up once you have decided on Telluride: where exactly the license comes from and when that office is open, which ceremony spots need a permit and from whom, what the gondola is doing on your date, and which of the famous dirt roads your rental car can actually drive. Every rule below was checked on the agency's own page in November 2026.

## The San Miguel County license

The San Miguel County Clerk and Recorder is at 305 W. Colorado Ave. in Telluride, on the first floor. Marriage licenses are issued on a walk in basis Monday through Thursday; the county's page gives the application hours as 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the office is closed Fridays. The fee is $30, payable by cash or check only, so bring one or the other because the counter does not take cards. Certified copies are $1.25 each, ordered in person or by mail with both names, the ceremony date and your mailing address.

Both of you appear with ID and the standard application details, and you can start the application online before you arrive. There is no waiting period, the license is valid for 35 days anywhere in Colorado, and the signed certificate goes back to this office within 63 days; the statewide rules and the absentee affidavit are in our [Colorado marriage license guide](/resources/colorado-marriage-license).

Because the office closes Fridays, a couple arriving Thursday night for a Saturday ceremony has a problem. Either land Wednesday, pick the license up in another county on the way (the Montrose County clerk if you fly into Montrose, after checking its hours, or any Front Range clerk from our [same day marriage guide](/resources/same-day-marriage-colorado)), or move the ceremony to a weekday.

Two notes on the town. Telluride books Town Park facilities, the Core Area and the Hanley Pavilion among them, for private events through a parks reservation, not a special event permit; the special event application is for events open to the public. Mountain Village is stricter on paper: its permit applies when two of three tests are met. A ceremony on a town plaza with a hired photographer meets two, town land plus the film and photography registration the town counts as a use of its resources. Plan on the $50 minor event permit filed 45 days out, with a certificate of insurance. A photographer working on town property in Mountain Village registers through the film and photography permit, which is free with seven days' notice.

For the forest sites, the Grand Mesa, Uncompahgre and Gunnison National Forests follow the national rule: a noncommercial group use permit applies at 75 or more participants or spectators. Below that, a ceremony with no built structures needs nothing, though a paid vendor working on forest land is technically a commercial use and should ask the Norwood Ranger District. Our [national forest elopement guide](/resources/national-forest-and-blm-elopements) explains the rule.

## Seasons: the gondola, the roads and the weather

The free gondola between Telluride and Mountain Village runs seven days a week from 6:30 a.m. to midnight and takes about 12 minutes. It closes for maintenance in the shoulder seasons. The Town of Mountain Village's current calendar: closed Oct. 26 through Nov. 19, 2026 for fall maintenance, winter season Nov. 20, 2026 through April 4, 2027, summer season May 20 through Oct. 24, 2027. Between seasons the town runs buses, which means no gondola ride to San Sophia and no sunset portraits at the top during the April gap or the late October one.

The dirt roads follow the snow. Alta Lakes Road (Forest Road 632) and Last Dollar Road are passable from roughly June through October; the Forest Service describes Alta as open with access that may be limited by conditions, and Visit Telluride reported the road reopened Aug. 20, 2025 after a summer of repairs. The Bridal Veil Falls road is closed in winter, and the tourism board discourages even hiking to the falls then because of rockfall and avalanche hazard. Ophir Pass is a summer road over scree at 12,789 feet.

The NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals for the Telluride 4 WNW station at 8,645 feet: January highs of 36 F and lows of 0 F with about 24 inches of snow; June highs of 72 F, lows of 34 F and rain on roughly one day in five, the driest month; July and August highs in the mid 70s with rain on two days in five, almost always after noon; late September highs of 68 F with 34 F mornings; November back to 45 F highs and 19 inches of snow. Our [month by month weather table](/resources/colorado-elopement-month-by-month) has the full twelve rows.

## Where to stay

Telluride proper is walkable to the clerk, the gondola and dinner, Mountain Village sits at the top of the gondola for a San Sophia ceremony, and Ridgway is the right base for a sunrise on Last Dollar Road because you start from the Highway 62 end. The [Telluride and the San Juans guide](/guides/telluride-san-juans) compares the four towns, including Ouray.

## A sample day

For a Thursday in the last week of September, with the license picked up Wednesday afternoon:

1. 5:30 a.m. Hair and makeup in Telluride.
2. 6:30 a.m. Drive out past the airport onto Last Dollar Road with your photographer; first light on the Sneffels Range about 7:00.
3. 7:15 a.m. Vows at a pullout in the aspens. Sign the certificate on the hood, fill in "Last Dollar Road, San Miguel County" as the place.
4. 9:00 a.m. Portraits down the road toward the Dallas Divide, breakfast in Ridgway.
5. 1:00 p.m. Back in Telluride; rest.
6. 4:30 p.m. Gondola to San Sophia for sunset photos of the box canyon.
7. 6:30 p.m. Dinner in town or at the top. Mail the certificate to the clerk Friday morning from the Telluride post office.

For a winter version, swap the morning for a 9 a.m. snowshoe at the Town Park trails or a ceremony at San Sophia at opening, and skip every dirt road on this page.

## Common questions

### Where do we get a marriage license in Telluride?

At the San Miguel County Clerk and Recorder, 305 W. Colorado Ave., Monday through Thursday on a walk in basis. The fee is $30 in cash or by check. The office is closed Fridays.

### Do we need a permit to elope at Bridal Veil Falls?

Not for a small ceremony at the base of the falls, which is reached by public road and trail. The power plant at the top is private property and is not open to visitors. Expect heavy summer foot and vehicle traffic by mid morning.

### Can we get married on the gondola or at the top?

The gondola is public transit, so there is no ceremony on it, but it is free and runs until midnight, which makes the top a natural portrait stop. The ceremony site at the top, San Sophia Overlook, is a Telluride Ski Resort venue booked through its wedding office and available June to October and December to March.

### When does the gondola close?

For the current season: Oct. 26 through Nov. 19, 2026 for fall maintenance, then daily from Nov. 20 through April 4, 2027, and again from May 20 through Oct. 24, 2027. The town runs buses in the gaps.
