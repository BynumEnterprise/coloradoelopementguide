The foothills west of Denver hold the most famous red rock in the state and a dozen quieter ceremony spots within forty minutes of the airport. The confusing part is that they belong to different governments. Red Rocks Park and Lookout Mountain Park are Denver Mountain Parks, owned by the City and County of Denver even though they sit in Jefferson County. Mount Falcon and Lair o' the Bear are Jefferson County Open Space. The state parks nearby belong to Colorado Parks and Wildlife. Each has its own permit, and asking the wrong office costs you weeks.

## Red Rocks, three ways

**As a wedding venue.** Red Rocks Park and Amphitheatre is owned and operated by the City and County of Denver, and its private events team books two wedding spaces. The Trading Post Backyard is a garden behind the Trading Post, below the amphitheatre, with views of the valley and the rock formations; Red Rocks lists it for formal ceremonies, with a stated maximum of 100 banquet or 200 reception. The Rock Room inside the Visitor Center is built around one of the red rocks, with sliding glass doors to a lower terrace; it is listed at 152 banquet or 250 reception. Pricing is by inquiry only, through the form on the Red Rocks weddings page. If you want chairs, catering and a weather backup at Red Rocks, this is the route.

**As a public park.** The rest of Red Rocks Park, outside the venue, is a Denver Mountain Park open to the public. A private, invitation only gathering in a Denver park is what the city calls a special occasion, handled by the Parks Permit Office rather than the venue. On concert days Denver requires its Red Rocks picnic shelter permit holders to leave by 4 p.m. for event traffic, groups must stay inside their permitted site or on designated trails, and climbing on the rock formations is prohibited. Plan any public park ceremony at Red Rocks for a non concert day or a morning.

**As a backdrop.** Two people and a photographer walking the trails at sunrise are visitors, not an event. The city's casual use rules for parks without a permit still apply: do not interfere with permitted groups or other visitors. Keep it small and quick.

## Denver Mountain Parks permits

The Denver Parks Permit Office handles special occasion permits for all Denver parks, including the Mountain Parks. What the city publishes:

- Applications go by email to park.permits@denvergov.org or in person at the Parks Permit Office, 6820 E. 12th Ave. in Denver.
- Submit no later than 30 days before your date. The office confirms receipt within one business day and sends a preliminary permit, which doubles as your invoice and requirements list, within seven business days.
- Applicants must be 18 or older. You may list several parks and dates in order of preference.
- For 2027, permit applications open on Monday, November 2, 2026, at 8 a.m. Mountain Time, by email only. Popular dates go fast, so include backups.

Fees are per day, per location. For an individual and a group of 1 to 350 people, Denver's 2026 fee schedule lists $260 without alcohol and $440 with alcohol, plus a separate setup and strike rate if you need extra time. Insurance, alcohol and noise rules have their own documents on the permit page.

If a picnic site is what you really want, Denver also rents Mountain Parks picnic sites from May 1 to September 30, 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., booked at least 48 hours ahead. Sheltered sites are $97 and non sheltered $51, with higher rates at Genesee and Newton parks. Mountain Parks have pit or portable toilets, limited parking and no water or electricity at most sites; you may not attach decorations to trees or structures; and Lookout Mountain Park picnic sites cannot be permitted on Memorial Day, July 4 or Labor Day. Ask the Parks Permit Office at 720-913-0700 which permit fits your plan.

## Lookout Mountain

Lookout Mountain rises directly above Golden with the city and plains spread out to the east. The summit area includes Lookout Mountain Park, a Denver Mountain Park under the permits above, and Boettcher Mansion, a historic Jefferson County property that the county itself suggests as a ceremony location. Boettcher is booked as a venue; see its [listing](/vendor/boettcher-mansion-lookout-mt) for contact details. The road up, Lariat Loop, is narrow and busy with cyclists on weekends, so give guests extra time.

## Mount Falcon and Lair o' the Bear

Both are Jefferson County Parks and Open Space properties. Mount Falcon, above Morrison, has the stone ruins of the Walker home and long views east and west; Lair o' the Bear is a creekside park in Bear Creek Canyon near Idledale, flat and shaded.

Jeffco's rule is general: any activity that conflicts with the park regulations needs a permit, including hosting an event or an organized or commercial activity. Its general use permit covers gatherings that benefit an individual or group rather than the public, and groups using a site not designed for concentrated group use. Read the county's Recreation and Activity Management Guide before you apply, and allow up to ten business days for a response.

Two Jeffco rules matter for elopements specifically. First, group size on trails is currently limited to 14 hikers, with groups separated by at least a quarter mile, and larger groups are not permitted. That makes a walk in ceremony at the Mount Falcon ruins a small group affair by rule. Second, professional photographers need a Jeffco photography permit, $125 for the calendar year; single day permits ended on January 1, 2026. Drones are prohibited. Ask your photographer whether they hold the annual permit.

If you want a reserved, covered spot, Jeffco rents shelters at Hiwan Heritage Park in Evergreen (the Grove, up to 68 people) and Pine Valley Ranch Park (from the Beahr Pagoda at 13 to the Bald Eagle shelter at 100), in five hour blocks, booked at least ten days ahead.

## State parks nearby

Roxborough, Golden Gate Canyon and Staunton state parks are all within an hour of Denver and all require a special activity agreement for a wedding. Our [state parks page](/resources/colorado-state-parks-elopements) covers the process.
