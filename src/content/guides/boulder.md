Boulder is where the plains end. The Flatirons tilt up out of the city's western edge, Chautauqua's meadow runs straight to their base, and Flagstaff Road climbs 2,000 feet in ten minutes to a summit with the whole Front Range behind you. For a couple flying in with a handful of guests, it is the shortest distance between an airport and a mountain ceremony in Colorado.

## Why elope here

Boulder manages its land carefully, which is good news for planning: the city publishes exactly what needs a permit, what it costs and what does not. You can marry on the Chautauqua lawn with your parents and a photographer and never fill out a form, or reserve a 1930s stone amphitheater on top of Flagstaff Mountain for an hour at dawn. The town's food, trails and walkable downtown handle the rest of the weekend.

## Best ceremony spots

**Chautauqua Park lawn.** The open meadow at 900 Baseline Road with the Flatirons filling the view. Groups under 50 need no permit and simply follow park rules; 50 to 100 people need the city's large-group lawn permit, which does not reserve a specific patch of grass. Paid parking applies on summer weekends and holidays, with a free shuttle from satellite lots.

**Sunrise Amphitheater, Flagstaff Mountain.** A circular stone amphitheater built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1933, at the east end of the summit beneath the flagpole, with Boulder and the plains below. Reservation only, through the city's Open Space and Mountain Parks, from May 1 through October 30. Dogs on leash are welcome; there is no electricity or water.

**Lost Gulch Overlook.** A pullout on Flagstaff Road above the amphitheater with a westward view over the foothills to the Indian Peaks and the Continental Divide. The classic Boulder sunset portrait stop. It is OSMP land, and OSMP allows weddings and elopements only at its designated shelters and facilities, so hold the vows at Sunrise Amphitheater or another reservable site and come here for photos afterward. The lot is small and crowded at golden hour, so go on a weekday.

**Eldorado Canyon State Park.** Ten miles south, a narrow canyon of golden sandstone walls with South Boulder Creek running through it and climbers overhead. A state park, so ceremonies run through a special activity agreement and every car needs a pass; summer weekends require a timed vehicle reservation.

**Brainard Lake Recreation Area.** An hour up the Peak to Peak Highway near Ward, a lake at 10,300 feet with the Indian Peaks reflected in it. National forest, with timed-entry reservations required in summer and fall. A small ceremony with no props needs no permit; the wilderness boundary beyond Long Lake limits group size.

**Flatirons Vista.** South of town off Highway 93, a short loop trail through grassland with the Flatirons and Eldorado's walls in profile. Quiet, open and easy walking, which makes it a good sunrise portrait spot with a stroller or a grandparent. It is OSMP land too, so the ceremony itself belongs at a reserved facility. Our guide to [Boulder elopement permits](/resources/boulder-elopement-permits) lists every reservable OSMP site.

## Permits and rules

The Chautauqua lawn is managed by Boulder Parks and Recreation. Groups of fewer than 50 do not need a permit. Groups of 50 to 100 apply for a large-group lawn permit, available up to a year ahead for ceremonies only, covering four hours between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m., with a $25 booking fee charged on approval. The city does not issue lawn permits after 5 p.m. because of events in the Chautauqua buildings. Rules and the booking link are on the [city's Chautauqua Park page](https://bouldercolorado.gov/locations/chautauqua-park).

Sunrise Amphitheater is reserved through the city's [Open Space and Mountain Parks facility rentals](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/shelter-rentals), up to two years ahead. One-hour blocks from 6 to 9 a.m. are $100 for Boulder residents and $150 for non-residents; three-hour blocks at 10 a.m., 2 p.m. and 6 p.m. are $300 and $400. Total attendance across all summit facilities is capped at 100 per time block, the amphitheater is not reservable from October 31 through April 30 when the summit road closes to cars, and vehicles not registered in Boulder County need a $5 daily parking permit. Alcohol needs a $25 city permit. Lost Gulch and the trails have no reservation system; commercial photographers on open space need their own city permit.

Eldorado Canyon requires a special activity agreement through [Colorado Parks and Wildlife](https://cpw.state.co.us/weddings-and-special-events). On the Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests, including Brainard Lake, non-commercial groups under 75 people without props generally need no permit; anything with chairs or an arch, or inside the Indian Peaks Wilderness, should be cleared with the [Boulder Ranger District](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/permits/events-weddings).

## Your marriage license

Any Colorado county clerk can issue a license that works statewide. You both appear with photo ID (a notarized absentee affidavit covers a partner who cannot), the fee is $30, there is no waiting period, blood test or witness requirement, and under C.R.S. 14-2-109 you may solemnize your own marriage. Use the license within 35 days and return the signed certificate to the issuing clerk within 63 days of the ceremony.

The Boulder County Clerk and Recorder issues licenses remotely, through an online application and a video appointment, with drop boxes in Boulder, Longmont and Lafayette for returning the signed certificate. Licenses are mailed only within Colorado, so out of state couples need to plan a pickup; our [Boulder County marriage license guide](/resources/boulder-county-marriage-license) walks through it.

## When to go

**December through February** is dry and bright more often than not, with Chinook winds that can gust over 70 miles an hour and snow that melts in days. Chautauqua and Flatirons Vista are open; the Flagstaff summit road is closed to cars.

**March and April** bring Boulder's heaviest, wettest snows, sometimes two feet overnight, followed by green grass a week later.

**May** reopens the summit road and the amphitheater. The meadow is at its greenest and the Flatirons hold a last dusting of snow.

**June through August** means warm mornings, afternoon thunderstorms that build over the Divide by 2 p.m., and heavy weekend crowds at Chautauqua. Brainard Lake opens by late June and peaks with wildflowers in July.

**September and October** are Boulder's finest weeks: dry, warm days, cottonwoods along the creek turning gold, aspens up the Peak to Peak in late September, and clear sunsets at Lost Gulch.

**November** is quiet, with the amphitheater closed and the lawn empty.

## Sunrise or sunset, and a sample day

Chautauqua faces the Flatirons to the west, so sunrise lights the rock in warm color while the meadow is still cool and empty. Lost Gulch faces west and belongs to sunset. The amphitheater on Flagstaff looks east over the plains, which makes its 6 a.m. hour the best value in the city.

A Thursday in late September:

1. 4:45 a.m. Hair and makeup at your rental near downtown.
2. 5:45 a.m. Drive up Flagstaff Road, fifteen minutes, with your parking permit displayed.
3. 6:30 a.m. Your amphitheater hour begins. Vows as the sun clears the horizon.
4. 7:30 a.m. Portraits at the summit, then down to Chautauqua's meadow.
5. 9:00 a.m. Breakfast on Pearl Street.
6. 6:15 p.m. Lost Gulch for sunset over the Divide, then dinner downtown.

## Where to stay

Boulder itself, especially near Pearl Street and the Hill, keeps everything walkable. The Chautauqua cottages, rented through the Colorado Chautauqua Association, put you a hundred yards from the lawn. Louisville and Longmont are cheaper and twenty minutes away. For Brainard Lake, stay in Nederland or Lyons.

## Getting there

Denver International is about 45 miles and 45 minutes from Boulder via E-470 and US 36. Flagstaff summit is a fifteen-minute drive from Chautauqua on a narrow road with a 30-foot vehicle limit and a snow-tire requirement from October through May. Brainard Lake is an hour from town.
