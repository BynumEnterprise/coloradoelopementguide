Northern Colorado is the understated choice. Fort Collins is a college town with a historic Old Town and more breweries than it needs, and ten minutes west the pavement ends at Horsetooth Reservoir, a six-mile lake tucked behind a red hogback. Beyond it the Cache la Poudre, Colorado's only Wild and Scenic river, cuts a canyon forty miles into the mountains. You will share most of it with locals, not tour buses.

## Why elope here

The region is close, cheap and uncrowded compared with Estes Park an hour up the road. Larimer County manages Horsetooth with a clear permit ladder, Lory State Park sits next door, and the Poudre Canyon offers river pullouts and lakes where a small ceremony needs nothing more than a sunrise. If you want granite and glaciers, drive to Rocky Mountain National Park. If you want a quiet morning on a ridge with the plains lit up below you and dinner in a real town, stay here.

## Best ceremony spots

**Horsetooth Rock.** The notched summit that gives the reservoir its name, 7,255 feet, reached by a steady two-and-a-half-mile climb from the Horsetooth Mountain Open Space trailhead. Sunrise from the top looks east over the reservoir to the plains and west to the Mummy Range. A hike-in ceremony for the fit, with a county entrance permit per vehicle.

**Horsetooth Reservoir overlooks and coves.** Along County Road 23 on the dam side, pullouts and day-use areas look across the water to the foothills. Rotary Park, South Bay and the Inlet Bay area are the easiest access, with county pavilions reservable for larger groups.

**Lory State Park.** Adjacent to Horsetooth's west shore, a state park of grassland valleys and the Arthur's Rock trail, which climbs to a granite outcrop with the whole reservoir below. Ceremonies run through the park's special activity agreement; the park also has a conference room for a rainy-day backup.

**The Poudre Canyon.** Highway 14 follows the river from Bellvue to Cameron Pass with picnic areas and pullouts the whole way. Along the Wild and Scenic corridor the Forest Service may ask for a permit even for small groups, so call first. Fall color peaks in the canyon's cottonwoods and aspens in late September.

**Red Feather Lakes and Dowdy Lake.** An hour northwest, a scatter of small lakes in the pines at 8,300 feet on the Canyon Lakes Ranger District. Quiet, cool in summer, and far from any crowd.

**Devil's Backbone Open Space.** West of Loveland, a serrated ridge of sandstone with a short loop trail through the Keyhole. Another Larimer County park with the same permit rules as Horsetooth, and a good choice for guests who cannot hike far.

## Permits and rules

Horsetooth Mountain, the reservoir parks and Devil's Backbone are managed by Larimer County Natural Resources, which handles ceremonies by size: small ceremonies of 20 or fewer go through a simple permit, receptions and larger groups reserve a group pavilion, and anything with more setup goes through the county's online special event application. Every vehicle needs a daily entrance permit, alcohol is restricted to campsites and boats, and event insurance may be required. Start at the county's [parks permits page](https://www.larimer.gov/dnrpermits) and call the Horsetooth information center with questions.

Lory State Park requires a special activity agreement and a vehicle pass; see [Colorado Parks and Wildlife](https://cpw.state.co.us/weddings-and-special-events). On the Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests, which cover the Poudre Canyon, Red Feather and Cameron Pass, non-commercial groups under 75 people without props generally need no permit, but the Forest Service flags the Cache la Poudre corridor and wilderness areas as places where a permit may apply, and anything with chairs, arches or paid vendors on site requires one. The [Forest Service events and weddings page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/permits/events-weddings) explains the test, and the Canyon Lakes Ranger District in Fort Collins answers the phone.

## Your marriage license

Any Colorado county clerk can issue a license that works statewide. You both appear with photo ID (a notarized absentee affidavit covers a partner who cannot), the fee is $30, there is no waiting period, blood test or witness requirement, and under C.R.S. 14-2-109 you may solemnize your own marriage. Use the license within 35 days and return the signed certificate to the issuing clerk within 63 days of the ceremony.

The Larimer County Clerk and Recorder's main recording office is in Fort Collins on West Oak Street, where walk-ins are welcome and appointments are encouraged; the Loveland and Estes Park offices issue licenses by appointment. You can start the application online and finish it in person. If you are coming from Greeley, the Weld County Clerk is there. Everything is on [larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage](https://www.larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage).

## When to go

**December through February** is dry and often sunny at 5,000 feet, with cold mornings and a reservoir that freezes at the edges. Horsetooth Rock is a snowy but doable hike with traction; the canyon above Poudre Park gets icy.

**March and April** bring the heaviest snow of the year to the foothills and wind on the ridges, followed by the first green on the hogback.

**May and early June** are the greenest weeks, with wildflowers at Lory and Devil's Backbone and the river running high and loud.

**July and August** are hot in town, with thunderstorms building over the foothills most afternoons and a busy reservoir on weekends. Mornings on the ridge are cool and clear.

**Late September and early October** are the best weeks: dry, golden cottonwoods in the canyon, aspens near Cameron Pass, and cool sunrises over the plains.

**November** is quiet and brown, with Cameron Pass getting its first real snow.

## Sunrise or sunset, and a sample day

Horsetooth faces east over the plains, so sunrise from the rock or the dam overlooks is the signature light, with the reservoir going from black to blue below you. The Poudre Canyon is shaded until mid-morning and glows in the late afternoon. Summer storms argue for mornings everywhere.

A late-September Saturday:

1. 4:30 a.m. Hair and makeup in Old Town Fort Collins.
2. 5:15 a.m. Drive to the Horsetooth Mountain trailhead, 25 minutes, entrance permit on the dash.
3. 5:30 a.m. Headlamps on, up the trail with your photographer.
4. 6:50 a.m. Sunrise from Horsetooth Rock. Vows with the plains lighting up.
5. 9:00 a.m. Back at the car, breakfast in Old Town.
6. 4:30 p.m. Drive up the Poudre Canyon for golden-hour portraits by the river, then dinner in Fort Collins.

## Where to stay

Old Town Fort Collins has the hotels, the restaurants and a 20-minute drive to Horsetooth. Loveland is quieter and closer to Devil's Backbone and the Big Thompson. Bellvue and the lower Poudre Canyon have cabins along the river, and Red Feather Lakes is the pines-and-silence option an hour out.

## Getting there

Denver International is about 65 miles and an hour and 15 minutes to Fort Collins via E-470 and I-25. Northern Colorado Regional Airport in Loveland has limited commercial service. A car is essential; Horsetooth is 20 minutes from Old Town and Cameron Pass is an hour and a half up the canyon.
