Estes Park is where most Colorado elopement stories start. The town sits at 7,500 feet with Longs Peak filling the southern sky, and the national park begins a few minutes past the last coffee shop. What trips couples up is not the scenery. It is the paperwork and the calendar, so this guide puts those first.

## Why elope here

Rocky Mountain National Park runs one of the most organized wedding permit programs in the country: clear rules, named sites, rangers who have seen everything. The limits are strict too: no decorations, no arches, two hours on site, a hard cap of 30 people. For a simple, honest ceremony with the Continental Divide behind you, it is hard to beat. For a floral installation and a string quartet, book a private venue in the Estes Valley and visit the park for portraits afterward.

## Best ceremony spots

Every ceremony inside the park must happen at a designated site. These are the ones couples ask about most, with the park's published group limits.

**3M Curve.** A pullout on US 36 between Upper Beaver Meadows and Deer Ridge Junction with a wide view across Moraine Park to Longs Peak. Fifteen people, three vehicles. The road stays open year round, which makes it the park's signature winter site. Expect wind.

**Sprague Lake.** A flat, accessible half-mile loop around a lake that reflects Hallett Peak. Fifteen people in summer, 30 in winter, because Bear Lake Road is jammed by 8 a.m. all summer. Sunrise only.

**Lily Lake.** Off Highway 7 south of town, outside the main entrance stations, with three permitted spots: the dock (10 people), the trail (20) and the south side picnic area (30). The dock is wheelchair accessible; the south side picnic area is up a rocky path and is not. All three have the postcard view of Longs Peak.

**Upper Beaver Meadows.** A broad aspen-edged meadow at the end of a dirt road. Thirty people. In late September the elk rut plays out here at dusk.

**Bear Lake.** Winter weekdays only, up to 20 people. Snow-covered, silent, and lit pink at first light.

Hidden Valley, Copeland Lake, the Moraine Park amphitheater, and Harbison Meadows and Timber Creek on the Grand Lake side round out the list. Overlooks, visitor centers and parking lots are never allowed, however small your group.

## Permits and rules

Any ceremony in the park requires a Special Use Permit. The administrative fee is $300, non-refundable, paid only after the park emails you a draft permit. Applications open exactly one year ahead of your wedding month and are reviewed in order received. The park caps permits at 60 ceremonies a month from May through October and 40 the rest of the year, with no more than two weddings per day at any site. As of this writing the park reports May, June, August, September and October 2026 fully booked, which tells you how early to start.

Your permit covers a two-hour window including setup and photos, and it doubles as your timed entry reservation. Entrance fees still apply to every vehicle. Decorations, tables, tents and anything scattered or released are prohibited; a few chairs for guests who cannot stand are fine. Drones are banned park wide. Afterward you are free to roam for portraits. The official page, with the application and per-site rules, is [nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/weddings.htm](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/weddings.htm).

Outside the park, Larimer County's Hermit Park Open Space reserves its pavilion and sites through [Larimer County Natural Resources](https://www.larimer.gov/dnrpermits). Along the Peak to Peak Highway, the Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests allow small ceremonies without a permit when the group is under 75 and you bring no props; [check with the ranger district](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/arp/permits/events-weddings) for wilderness areas or anything with chairs or an arch.

## Your marriage license

Colorado makes this part easy. Any county clerk and recorder in the state can issue a license that works anywhere in Colorado. You both appear with photo ID (one partner can sign a notarized absentee affidavit if needed), the fee is $30, there is no waiting period or blood test, and witnesses are not required. Under C.R.S. 14-2-109 you may solemnize your own marriage, so the two of you can sign as your own officiants. The license must be used within 35 days of issue, and the signed certificate goes back to the issuing clerk within 63 days of the ceremony.

The closest office is the Larimer County Clerk and Recorder in Estes Park, by appointment only, so book ahead; Fort Collins and Loveland also issue licenses. On the Grand Lake side, the Grand County Clerk is in Hot Sulphur Springs. Our [Larimer County marriage license guide](/resources/larimer-county-marriage-license) has each office's hours and the ID list. Hours and the online pre-application are at [larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage](https://www.larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage).

## When to go

**January through March** is the quiet season. Trail Ridge Road is closed past Many Parks Curve, but 3M Curve, Sprague Lake, Bear Lake and Lily Lake are all reachable. Expect single-digit mornings, wind and empty lots. Permits are easy to get.

**April and May** are mud season: snow above 9,000 feet, frozen lakes into May, spring storms that drop a foot overnight.

**June** brings the opening of Trail Ridge Road, usually around Memorial Day weekend, and the start of timed entry. High meadows hold snow into mid-month. Afternoon thunderstorms begin.

**July and August** are peak everything: wildflowers above treeline, storms most afternoons by 1 p.m., crowds that fill Bear Lake Road by dawn. Sunrise is logistics, not a flourish.

**September and early October** are the best weeks of the year for most couples. Aspens turn in the last two weeks of September, the elk rut fills the meadows at dusk, mornings are crisp and afternoons usually dry. Trail Ridge Road typically closes in mid-October.

**November and December** swing between golden late fall and full winter, with a 7 a.m. sunrise and a 4:30 p.m. sunset.

## Sunrise or sunset, and a sample day

Sunrise wins here almost every time: the light hits the peaks from the east, the lakes are glass before the wind, the storms have not built, the crowds are asleep. Sunset works in winter and at Upper Beaver Meadows in fall, but the Divide goes into shadow early.

A late-September day with a sunrise permit at Sprague Lake:

1. 4:45 a.m. Hair and makeup at your rental. Pack the printed permit and license.
2. 6:15 a.m. Arrive at Sprague Lake. Your window opens at 6:30.
3. 6:50 a.m. First light on Hallett Peak. Vows at the east shore.
4. 7:30 a.m. Portraits around the loop, then up Trail Ridge Road.
5. 9:30 a.m. Breakfast in town. Nap.
6. 5:30 p.m. Upper Beaver Meadows for elk at dusk and more photos.

## Where to stay

Estes Park has the most options and the shortest drive to every site; cabins along Fall River Road and the Big Thompson are quieter than downtown. Allenspark, 20 minutes south, is closest to Lily Lake and Wild Basin with far fewer people. Lyons, down the canyon, offers a small-town main street and an easier run to Denver. For Harbison Meadows or Timber Creek, stay in Grand Lake: two hours across Trail Ridge Road in summer, three and a half around it in winter.

## Getting there

Denver International is the gateway: about 75 miles and an hour and 45 minutes to Estes Park via E-470 and US 36 through Lyons, longer on summer weekends. From Boulder it is an hour. Rent a car; in winter ask for all-wheel drive and check [Trail Ridge Road status](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/trail_ridge_road.htm) that morning.
