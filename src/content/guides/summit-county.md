Summit County is the high country most people picture: Breckenridge's Victorian main street, Frisco's marina, the Tenmile Range over a reservoir that turns sapphire in July and white in January. It is also the most convenient real mountain elopement in the state. You can land at Denver International at noon and be at 9,500 feet by three, which is exactly why the popular spots need a reservation and a sunrise alarm.

## Why elope here

Three things make Summit work for small weddings. Access: Swan Mountain Road, Loveland Pass and the resort gondolas run most of the year, so winter elopements are realistic. Proximity: the towns sit within twenty minutes of each other. And certainty: Sapphire Point is a genuinely reservable site, which removes the guesswork that haunts dispersed Forest Service locations.

## Best ceremony spots

**Sapphire Point Overlook.** A short loop trail on Swan Mountain Road between Keystone and Breckenridge leads to a rocky point with Dillon Reservoir below and the Gore and Tenmile ranges beyond. The Forest Service calls it the only designated wedding site on the Dillon Ranger District. It is reserved in three hour blocks for up to 30 people, it is non exclusive (hikers will walk through), reserved events get just two parking spaces, and chairs are not allowed. From June 14, 2026 all reservations use the new north overlook. Our [Sapphire Point guide](/resources/sapphire-point-overlook-elopement) has every rule.

**Loveland Pass.** The summit of US 6 sits on the Continental Divide at 11,990 feet, with a pullout, the sign everyone photographs and a short ridge walk to views of Arapahoe Basin. There is nothing to book. The east side is Arapaho National Forest's Clear Creek district and the west side is the White River's Dillon district, so a tiny ceremony with no props falls under general Forest Service group rules, but because the Dillon district points couples to Sapphire Point, call before you plan around it. Expect wind, thin air, snow in any month and company on weekends.

**Resort ceremony decks.** Keystone, Breckenridge, Copper Mountain and Arapahoe Basin each run wedding venues with mountaintop ceremony spots reached by lift, booked through the resort. They cost real money, but they solve chairs, weather backup and dinner in one contract.

**Frisco and Dillon lakefronts.** Frisco Bay Marina, the Frisco Peninsula and Dillon's lakeside parks and amphitheater are town property, and each town issues its own special event permits. Check with the Town of Frisco or Town of Dillon before planning more than a two-person stroll.

## Permits and rules

Sapphire Point reservations are made on [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/venues/VR22273), where the listing shows a fee of about $120 to $142 per block. Summer weekend sunrise blocks go the morning they open, so set a reminder. Your reservation does not close the site to the public and does not allow amplified sound, chairs, food or drink, tents, thrown petals or anything staked into the ground. Recreation.gov also notes that commercial use at the site is prohibited, so ask your vendors how they work there and call the Dillon Ranger District if anything is unclear.

Elsewhere on national forest land, the rule of thumb is that non-commercial groups under 75 people without props generally do not need a permit, while anything with structures, chairs or a paid vendor on site may. The Dillon Ranger District in Silverthorne (970-468-5400) decides, and the [White River National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver) posts closures. Drones are prohibited in the Eagles Nest Wilderness.

## Your marriage license

Any Colorado county clerk can issue your license and it works statewide. You both appear with photo ID (an absentee affidavit covers a partner who cannot), the fee is $30, there is no waiting period, blood test or witness requirement, and under C.R.S. 14-2-109 you may solemnize your own marriage. The license must be used within 35 days of issue, and the signed certificate is returned to the issuing clerk within 63 days of the ceremony.

The Summit County Clerk and Recorder is in Breckenridge, a few blocks off Main Street, and non residents need one of four daily appointment slots; our [Summit County license page](/resources/summit-county-marriage-license) explains. Flying into Eagle County? The Eagle County Clerk has offices in Eagle and El Jebel, and Clear Creek County's clerk in Georgetown is on the way up from Denver. The statewide rules are laid out on any clerk's page, such as [Larimer County's](https://www.larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage), and your clerk hands you self-solemnization instructions with the license.

## When to go

**December through March** is deep winter at 9,000 feet. Sapphire Point is reachable and snow-draped, the reservoir is frozen white, and Loveland Pass is open except during storms and avalanche control. Highs in the 20s, and the highest lodging prices of the year. Our [winter elopement guide](/resources/winter-elopement-colorado) covers the traction law and which sites stay open.

**April and May** are the shoulder. Resorts close in mid to late April, trails turn to mud and the towns go quiet. Lodging is cheap and dates are easy.

**June** is green-up. Snow lingers on north slopes and passes into mid-month, wildflowers start low, afternoon storms begin. Boreas Pass Road usually opens late in the month.

**July and August** bring wildflowers up high, a thunderstorm most days between 1 and 4 p.m., and the biggest crowds. Sapphire Point weekends book out six months ahead. Sunrise is your friend.

**September** is the month locals get married. Aspens peak in the last two weeks, best seen along Boreas Pass Road south of Breckenridge, afternoons dry out and the first snow dusts the Tenmile Range. Early October can be gorgeous or wintry in the same week.

**November** is the quietest month, between leaf drop and ski season, with short days and real cold.

## Sunrise or sunset, and a sample day

Sapphire Point faces west over the reservoir, which makes it a classic sunset spot, but sunset is also when the lot is fullest and storms linger. Sunrise gives you alpenglow on the Gore Range behind the water and an empty trail. Loveland Pass is a sunrise site, full stop; afternoon wind up there will take your veil to Kansas.

A July day built around a 6 to 9 a.m. Sapphire Point block:

1. 4:15 a.m. Hair and makeup in Frisco or Breckenridge.
2. 5:30 a.m. Depart. Swan Mountain Road is fifteen minutes from either town.
3. 5:50 a.m. Walk the loop as the sky lightens.
4. 6:15 a.m. Vows as first light hits the peaks across the water.
5. 7:15 a.m. Portraits, then pack out by 8 sharp.
6. 8:30 a.m. Loveland Pass before the wind builds.
7. 10:30 a.m. Brunch in Frisco, then the Breckenridge dinner you booked weeks ago.

Loveland Pass has its own page: [the Loveland Pass elopement guide](/resources/loveland-pass-elopement) covers parking, wind, winter closures and Forest Service rules at the summit.

## Where to stay

Breckenridge has the widest range of lodging and the best walkable dinner scene, at the highest prices. Frisco is the local favorite: a real main street, the marina, and the shortest drive to Sapphire Point. Dillon and Silverthorne are quieter and cheaper with the same views. Keystone is resort condos, convenient if you are marrying on the mountain.

## Getting there

From Denver International, take I-70 west through the Eisenhower Tunnel to Frisco: about 90 miles and an hour and 45 minutes without traffic, which on winter weekends and summer Sunday afternoons is a big without. Eagle County Regional Airport in Gypsum is an hour west with seasonal direct flights. Loveland Pass is the scenic alternative to the tunnel and the hazmat route, so expect slow trucks. In winter the traction law applies on I-70; all-wheel drive is worth it.
