Telluride is a Victorian mining town at the dead end of a box canyon, with 13,000-foot walls on three sides and a free gondola up to Mountain Village. North over the Dallas Divide are Ridgway and Ouray; south over Lizard Head Pass is Durango. Nothing here is on the way to anything, which is why couples who make the drive tend to get the mountains to themselves.

## Why elope here

The San Juans are the most dramatic range in Colorado, and the aspen groves below the Sneffels Range are the ones you have seen in calendars. Telluride is small enough to walk, serious about its restaurants, and used to film-festival logistics, so vendors here are unflappable. The trade-offs: a long drive from Denver, a tiny weather-prone airport, and a town that limits private events in its parks.

## Best ceremony spots

**San Sophia Overlook.** At the top of the gondola between Telluride and Mountain Village, the ski resort maintains a ceremony site with the box canyon below and Wilson Peak to the west. It is booked through Telluride Ski Resort's wedding office, which handles chairs and the ride up.

**Bridal Veil Falls.** Colorado's tallest free-falling waterfall, 365 feet, at the head of the canyon. From the Pandora Mill lot it is a rough road and a two-mile climb to the base, which is public; the power plant at the top is private. No reservation system, heavy summer foot traffic by mid-morning, and spray that will soak a dress in the June melt.

**Alta Lakes.** A string of alpine lakes at 11,200 feet below Wilson Peak, reached by a rough dirt road from Highway 145 south of town, passable in a high-clearance vehicle from about mid June to October. Dispersed national forest with no reservations, so keep the group small and leave it as you found it.

**Last Dollar Road.** The dirt road between Telluride's airport mesa and the Dallas Divide winds through the densest aspen groves in the region with the Sneffels Range in front of you. Pullouts are public, the ranchland beside them is not. Open roughly June through October.

**Dallas Divide.** The Highway 62 pullout west of Ridgway with the postcard view of Mount Sneffels over ranch fences. It is a highway shoulder: be quick, stay on the public side of the fence, and expect photographers at every sunrise in late September.

**Ridgway State Park.** The overlook at the visitor center holds up to 100 guests with the San Juans across the reservoir, reserved through the park with a special activity agreement. The most practical option for a group that includes grandparents.

Over the Dallas Divide, Ouray has its own ceremony sites, park rentals and a weekday only clerk; our [Ouray elopement guide](/resources/ouray-elopement) covers them.

## Permits and rules

The Town of Telluride does not allow private functions such as weddings at the Town Park stage, the pocket parks or on town streets. Short events in the Town Park core area or pavilion may be approved administratively, with a facility application filed at least 45 days ahead; details are on the [town's Town Park facilities page](https://telluride-co.gov/Facilities/Facility/Details/-20) and applications run through [permits.telluride.gov](https://permits.telluride.gov/). For most couples the answer is simpler: hold the ceremony outside town limits and come back for dinner.

Bridal Veil Falls, Alta Lakes and the roads above town are a mix of national forest, county road and private mining land. On the Grand Mesa, Uncompahgre and Gunnison National Forests, non-commercial groups under 75 people without props generally do not need a permit; anything with structures or paid vendors should be cleared with the [Norwood Ranger District](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/gmug). State parks require a special activity agreement and a vehicle pass; see [Colorado Parks and Wildlife](https://cpw.state.co.us/weddings-and-special-events). The City of Ouray takes reservations for Box Cañon Falls Park, so ask the city before planning a ceremony there. Drones are restricted at most of these sites, and the Telluride Film Festival over Labor Day weekend and Bluegrass in June fill every bed and permit slot in town.

## Your marriage license

Any Colorado county clerk can issue a license that works statewide. You both appear with photo ID (a notarized absentee affidavit covers a partner who cannot), the fee is $30, there is no waiting period, blood test or witness requirement, and under C.R.S. 14-2-109 you may solemnize your own marriage. Use the license within 35 days and return the signed certificate to the issuing clerk within 63 days of the ceremony.

The San Miguel County Clerk and Recorder is on Colorado Avenue in Telluride. The Ouray County Clerk is in Ouray, the Montrose County Clerk is in Montrose if you fly into that airport, and the La Plata County Clerk is in Durango. Statewide rules are spelled out on any clerk's page, such as [Larimer County's](https://www.larimer.gov/clerk/recording/marriage).

## When to go

**December through March** is ski season at 8,750 feet. Town is lively, the high roads are closed, and the gondola runs daily, which makes San Sophia the realistic winter site.

**April and May** are mud season. The resort closes in early April, many restaurants take a break, and the backroads are snowbound. Lodging is cheapest.

**June** opens Last Dollar Road and the lower lakes, with snow on the peaks and the Bluegrass festival filling town for a long weekend mid-month.

**July and August** bring wildflowers at Alta Lakes, the high passes open, and thunderstorms most afternoons. Mornings are reliably clear.

**Late September** is the reason to come: the Sneffels aspens peak in the last week of the month and the first of October, Last Dollar Road glows, and lodging fills with photographers. Book a weekday.

**October and November** are quiet and crisp until snow closes the backroads, usually by late October.

## Sunrise or sunset, and a sample day

The Sneffels Range faces north, so it lights beautifully at both ends of the day, but the Dallas Divide and Last Dollar Road are sunrise sites because the light comes from the east and the photographers are already there. The box canyon itself catches late light on its west walls, so a sunset ceremony at San Sophia works. Afternoon storms in summer argue for morning everywhere.

A late-September weekday:

1. 5:00 a.m. Hair and makeup in Telluride or Ridgway.
2. 6:15 a.m. Drive Last Dollar Road from the Telluride side with your photographer.
3. 7:00 a.m. First light on Sneffels. Vows in an aspen grove at a pullout.
4. 8:30 a.m. Portraits down to the Dallas Divide.
5. 10:30 a.m. Breakfast in Ridgway, back to Telluride for a nap.
6. 5:30 p.m. Gondola to San Sophia for sunset photos, then dinner in town.

## Where to stay

Telluride proper is the most walkable and the most expensive; Mountain Village is condos and resort hotels connected by the free gondola. Ridgway, 45 minutes north, is the value base with the best access to Last Dollar Road and the Divide. Ouray is a hot-springs town in its own box canyon, 15 minutes beyond Ridgway. Durango, two hours south, works if you are flying in there and want a bigger town.

## Getting there

Telluride Regional Airport sits on a mesa 15 minutes from town, with small planes and frequent weather diversions. Montrose Regional is the reliable choice, an hour and a half north with direct flights from several hubs in ski season. Durango-La Plata is two hours south over Lizard Head Pass, and Grand Junction is two and a half hours. From Denver International it is six and a half hours by car. All-wheel drive matters from October through May, and the high dirt roads need clearance any time.
